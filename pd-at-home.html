APD, explained simply36 seconds · No audio needed

A general explanation of APD—not operating instructions for the RYNOR machine.

Read the video transcript
  1. PD uses the lining inside your belly to help remove waste and extra fluid from your blood.
  2. APD uses a machine called a cycler to move prescribed dialysis fluid through a soft catheter.
  3. Fresh fluid fills the belly, stays for a prescribed time, then drains away with waste. The cycle repeats.
  4. Treatment often runs overnight at home. Your kidney team decides the schedule and trains you or a helper.
  5. Home treatment may reduce travel for routine dialysis. Supplies, follow-up visits and a daily care routine are still needed.
  6. PD is not suitable for everyone. Infection and discomfort can occur. Ask your kidney-care team about benefits, risks and costs.

Start with the basics

One exchange.
Three simple ideas.

PD stands for peritoneal dialysis. The peritoneum is the lining inside your belly. Blood stays inside your body; dialysis fluid helps collect waste and extra water across this lining.

Think about your everyday life

Potential benefits.
Practical responsibilities.

01

Less travel for routine treatment

Home PD may reduce travel to a centre for routine dialysis. Clinic reviews and tests are still needed.

02

A routine planned around you

APD often happens while you sleep. Treatment is usually daily, and some people also need a daytime exchange.

03

A catheter, rather than repeated needles

Daily exchanges use a catheter placed during a procedure. PD is not guaranteed to be painless; discomfort or pain can occur.

What to discuss with your kidney team

Suitability, catheter placement, infection prevention, training, home space, electricity, supply storage, follow-up and the full cost of care. A helper may be needed. Costs and available support vary.

Questions people ask

Clear answers, before you decide.

Is PD suitable for everyone?

No. A kidney specialist considers your health, home situation, ability to carry out treatment and available support. This website and its learning activities cannot decide suitability.

Is APD the same as CAPD?

Both are types of peritoneal dialysis. APD uses a cycler, often overnight. CAPD uses manual exchanges. Your care team helps you choose and prescribes your treatment.

Will it save money or remove every hospital visit?

Home PD may reduce travel for routine treatment. Total costs depend on treatment, supplies, training, follow-up and local support. You still need clinical monitoring. RYNOR has not announced APD system prices or verified savings. Contact us for current CAPD supply prices.

What about pain and infection?

PD can cause discomfort, and infection is an important risk. Training and clean practice matter. If you are already on PD, follow your team’s instructions and seek prompt clinical advice for concerning symptoms such as abdominal pain, fever or cloudy drained fluid.

Can I buy the RYNOR system or join a trial here?

Not at present. RYNOR is preparing for clinical trials. This page records interest in education and future contact; it is not trial enrolment or a product order.
