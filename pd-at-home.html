PD or haemodialysis?

Treatment that fits around your life.

Peritoneal dialysis is done every day, 365 days a year. With APD, most of it happens at home while you sleep, so your days stay free for work, family and the things you enjoy.

Treatment at home

No travelling to a centre three times a week. You have regular clinic reviews instead.

No needles

PD uses a soft catheter in the belly, so there are no needles in a vein at each session.

Gentle and steady

Dialysis happens every day, rather than in a few large sessions, so the body’s fluid and waste levels change more gradually.

Often fewer diet and fluid limits

Many people on PD have fewer food and drink restrictions than with centre haemodialysis. A dietitian still guides you.

May protect remaining kidney function

Studies suggest PD may help keep the kidney function you still have for longer.

More freedom to plan

Your schedule is not tied to centre slots. With planning and supplies arranged, trips and family events can be easier.

More control

After training, you or a helper manage treatment in your own home, with your kidney team supporting you.

With home PD

  • Dialysis at home, mostly overnight while you sleep
  • Days free for work, studies and family
  • No needles; a soft catheter instead
  • Gentle, daily dialysis instead of big swings between sessions
  • Often fewer food and fluid limits
  • May help protect remaining kidney function
  • Clinic reviews instead of trips three times a week

With centre haemodialysis, many people face

  • Travel to a centre about 3 times a week, every week
  • Sessions of about 4 hours at fixed times, often in the working day
  • Needles into a fistula or graft at every session
  • Stricter fluid and diet limits, as fluid and waste build up between sessions
  • Feeling tired or washed out after a session
  • A long gap over the weekend, when fluid and waste build up most
  • Access problems, such as a blocked or infected fistula, graft or line

Haemodialysis is still the right choice for many people. Your kidney team will advise.

Every day, all year

PD is not a few sessions a week. It is part of every day, including weekends and holidays. It also needs a catheter, clean daily routines, space to store supplies, electricity at night for APD, and training. Infection of the belly lining (peritonitis) is an important risk, so cleanliness matters. PD is not suitable for everyone.

Questions people ask

Clear answers, before you decide.

Is PD suitable for everyone?

No. A kidney specialist considers your health, home situation, ability to carry out treatment and available support. This website and its learning activities cannot decide suitability.

Is APD the same as CAPD?

Both are types of peritoneal dialysis. APD uses a cycler, often overnight. CAPD uses manual exchanges. Your care team helps you choose and prescribes your treatment.

Will it save money or remove every hospital visit?

Home PD may reduce travel for routine treatment. Total costs depend on treatment, supplies, training, follow-up and local support. You still need clinical monitoring. RYNOR has not announced APD system prices or verified savings.

What about pain and infection?

PD can cause discomfort, and infection is an important risk. Training and clean practice matter. If you are already on PD, follow your team’s instructions and seek prompt clinical advice for concerning symptoms such as abdominal pain, fever or cloudy drained fluid.
